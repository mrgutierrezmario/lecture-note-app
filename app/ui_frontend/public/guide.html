1. Getting started

  1. Sign in with your username or email address. If you don't have an account, ask the person who runs this installation to create one.
  2. Type a name for the lecture in the box at the top (for example, MGT-699 Week 3). You can rename it later from History.
  3. Optional: click the Aa button next to the name for Lecture details: key terms (the professor's name, acronyms, technical words — the transcriber uses them to spell those right) and a notes focus (what the notes should emphasise, e.g. "formulas and worked examples"). Both can be changed during the lecture.
  4. Click Enable microphone and allow access when the browser asks. Pick the right microphone from the list if you have more than one.
  5. Click Start recording.
Browsers only allow the microphone over a secure (https) address, which this site uses. If the microphone is blocked, the button changes to Microphone blocked — how to fix and walks you through re-enabling it.

2. Recording a lecture

While recording, three panels work for you:

  • Live transcript — speech appears as text a few seconds after it is spoken. It follows along while you're at the bottom; scroll up to re-read something and it stays put, with a Jump to latest button to catch up.
  • Notes — every couple of minutes the new transcript is turned into structured notes: headings, key points, definitions, action items and deadlines. The notes grow and refine as the lecture goes on; the version number in the corner increases each time.
  • Ask about the lecture — a chat that answers from the transcript, the notes and anything you have added (see Asking questions).

Controls on the toolbar:

  • Pause / Resume — freeze the recording during a break and pick up exactly where you left off. The lecture stays open; nothing is finalised until you press Stop.
  • Mute mic — keep recording but silence the microphone (a private conversation). The transcript pauses and continues the moment you unmute.
  • Don't keep audio — transcribe without storing the recording. Use it when you only want the text: nothing counts toward your storage, but there is no MP3 afterwards. Set it before you press Start.
  • Tab audio (computers only) — also record the sound of a browser tab, such as a video or an online class running in the browser. See the next section.
If the connection drops mid-lecture, keep going. The status pill shows Buffering and the audio is held in your browser, then sent when the connection is back. Nothing is lost.

3. Online classes and calls

On a computer: if the class runs in a browser tab (Zoom in the browser, a recorded video, a stream), turn on Tab audio before starting. In the share dialog choose Chrome Tab, select the tab, and tick Share tab audio. Both the tab and your microphone are recorded.

On a phone: phones only let apps hear your side of a call, never the other participants. For a phone or video call, put the call on speakerphone so the microphone picks up the room, or record from a second device.

Zoom desktop app or other software (Mac): a virtual audio device such as BlackHole or Loopback can carry the meeting's sound into the recorder. Route Zoom's speaker output to the virtual device, select that device as the app's input, and record. Two things to know:

  • The app's Mute switch silences the selected input — with BlackHole selected, that is the meeting audio (the professor), not you. To mute yourself in the class, use Zoom's own mute; it doesn't affect the recording.
  • Your own voice is only recorded if it is part of the input. To capture your questions too, create an Aggregate Device in Audio MIDI Setup (your microphone + BlackHole) and select that instead.
If no sound is reaching the recorder for a while, the app tells you. On a call, the call usually owns the microphone — speakerphone or a second device fixes it.

4. Adding slides and documents

Drop a file onto Add slides to this lecture or click Browse:

  • PDF, PowerPoint (.pptx), Word (.docx) — the text is extracted.
  • Images (a photo of the whiteboard, a screenshot of a slide) — the text and any diagrams are read by an AI vision model.

Everything you add becomes part of what the chat can answer from. You can add materials during the lecture or afterwards.

5. Asking questions

Type a question in Ask about the lecture — for example, “What are the deadlines?”, “Summarise the part about pricing”, “Explain the second diagram”. Answers come only from this lecture's content, so if something wasn't covered the assistant says so rather than guessing.

  • Follow-up questions work: “When is that due?” after asking about a project.
  • Paste an image (Ctrl/⌘ + V) or drop one into the chat to ask about it.
  • The small label under each answer shows which AI model replied.
  • Questions and answers stay with the lecture: reopen it from History and they are still there, and the PDF/Word export includes them. Hover a message and click × to remove that question with its answer (a wrong turn you don't want in the export); the bin icon in the panel header clears the whole conversation.

6. After the lecture

Click Stop. A final notes pass runs, and the toolbar buttons let you download:

  • Notes — a Markdown file (opens in any text editor, Notion, Obsidian, etc.).
  • Transcript — a plain-text file with timestamps.
  • PDF and Word — one formatted document with the notes, your questions and the answers, and the full transcript (grouped into short paragraphs with times). The PDF is the one to print or share; the Word file is the one to edit.
  • MP3 — the whole recording as one audio file (unless you chose Don't keep audio). Preparing it takes a moment for long lectures; a spinner shows progress.

Editing the notes: the pencil in the Notes panel opens them for editing; saving creates a new version, and later passes build on your edit rather than overwriting it. On a finished lecture, the sparkle button regenerates the notes from the whole transcript — useful after setting a notes focus in Lecture details.

Stopped too early? Press Continue recording and the new audio is added to the same lecture — transcript and notes carry on. You can also come back to any lecture later from History to download the files, ask more questions, or add materials.

7. History and storage

The clock icon in the top bar opens Your lectures: every recording with a transcript, newest first. From each row you can open it, download, rename, keep, delete the audio only, or delete the lecture.

  • Transcripts and notes are kept indefinitely.
  • Audio recordings are deleted automatically after the number of days shown at the bottom of History. Download the MP3 before then if you want it, or…
  • Keep (the padlock) protects a lecture: its audio is never cleaned up and it can't be deleted until you unlock it. Each user can keep a limited number of lectures.
  • Storage — the bar at the top of History shows how much retained audio you have against your allowance. If you reach it, delete audio from old lectures or record with Don't keep audio.

8. Google Drive

Keep your own copy of every lecture. In Settings → Google Drive, click Connect Google Drive and allow access. The app asks only for permission to manage the files it creates itself — it cannot see anything else in your Drive.

  • With Save each lecture automatically on (the default), the notes, transcript, MP3 and the PDF and Word documents are uploaded when a recording stops.
  • For any past lecture, use History → Download → Save to Google Drive. Saving again updates the same files rather than making duplicates.
  • Choose where they go: type a name or path such as School/Fall 2026 and Create folder, or Choose existing folder to pick one you already have — Google's own chooser opens, and the app gets access to that one folder only. (If the button isn't there, the administrator hasn't enabled the folder selector.) You can also move the folder anywhere in your Drive; the app follows it.
  • Disconnect at any time; files already in your Drive stay yours.

9. Using it on a phone

  1. Open the site in Chrome (Android) or Safari (iPhone).
  2. Use Add to Home screen from the browser menu. It installs like an app, with the panels stacked vertically.
  3. The first tap on Enable microphone or Start recording asks for microphone permission — allow it. If you declined by mistake: long-press the app icon → App info → Permissions → Microphone.

While recording, the app keeps the screen awake so the phone doesn't suspend it mid-lecture — leave the app in the foreground; switching to another app or locking the phone can stop the recording (a limit of phone browsers, iPhones especially). Everything else works the same as on a computer, except Tab audio, which phones don't support. For calls, see Online classes and calls.

10. Your account

  • Settings → Appearance — light, dark, or follow your system.
  • Settings → Your account — change your email address or password.
  • Forgot password? on the sign-in page emails you a reset link.
  • Delete account (bottom of Your account) removes your account and every lecture in it, permanently, after confirming your password.
